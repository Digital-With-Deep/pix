NextSteps surfaces a short list of suggested follow-ups — things that are worth doing but
aren't blocking, unlike the required actions in `GettingStarted`. Each item is a finding
written as a statement, a short category chip for scanning, and at most one action link that
leads somewhere.

## When to use

- After the required setup in `GettingStarted` is finished, for the next tier of
  worthwhile-but-optional actions: rotating a key, inviting a missing teammate, turning on a
  notification.
- Keep `items` short — three or four is plenty. This is a nudge, not an inbox.
- Give each item at most one `action`; if an item needs two destinations, it's probably two
  items.
- Not for required, blocking setup — use `GettingStarted`, where steps track `done` state.
  Not for a single finding tied to specific evidence — use `Alert`.

## Basic

A chip (`kind` / `kindTone`) marks what kind of finding each item is, followed by the
statement (`title`), supporting detail (`body`), and an optional action link. An item with no
`action` just reads as informational.

## With a subtitle and dismiss

Pass `subtitle` to show provenance under the title — where the suggestions came from, or when
they were last refreshed. Pass `onDismiss` to show a header control (`dismissLabel`, default
"Dismiss all") for clearing the whole list at once.

Pass `loading` while the suggestions are still being computed. The card renders a skeleton in

## Anatomy

- `title` — the card heading. Defaults to "Suggested next steps".
- `subtitle` — an optional provenance line under the title, e.g. "3 actions from this week's
  sync · reviewed by the control agent".
- `items` — an array of `NextStepsItem`:
  - `kind` / `kindTone` — a short uppercase chip ("DRIFT", "GAP", "ATTEST") and its tone
    (`neutral`, `warning`, `danger`, `info`, `success`).
  - `title` — the finding, written as a statement of what is true.
  - `body` — why it matters and what has to happen.
  - `action` / `onAction` — the label and handler for the one action this item leads to.
- `onDismiss` / `dismissLabel` — shows a header control to clear the whole list; the label
  defaults to "Dismiss all".

## Accessibility

- Each item's `action` renders as a real `<button>`, so it's keyboard-focusable and operable
  by default; give it a label that stands on its own out of context (for example "Rotate key"
  rather than "Fix").
- `kindTone` colors the chip background and text together, not the background alone — check
  that the pairing still meets contrast if you introduce a new tone.
- The header `onDismiss` control is also a plain button; its visible label already states the
  action, so no extra `aria-label` is needed.
- While `loading`, the rendered skeleton has no text content — keep loading windows short so
  a screen reader isn't left announcing nothing.
