A stage flow lays out sequential stages side by side — intake, triage,
review, resolution — each showing the population at that stage, a one-line
note on what's true of items there, and a thin progress rule. It's built to
answer "where does the estate stand right now, and where does it stall,"
not to replay history.

## When to use

Use `StageFlow` for a population moving through a known, ordered pipeline —
a control lifecycle, a triage funnel, a review queue — where the point is
comparing stage-to-stage volume and spotting where things stick. Give a
stalled stage `tone="alert"` to tint it amber, and an unreached stage
`tone="idle"` to grey it out; both are deliberate overrides of the default,
which otherwise derives from whether `value` is zero. Prefer `CoverageMatrix`
when the shape of the data is a static grid of states rather than a
sequence, and `StageFlow`'s `compact` mode when it needs to sit as a tight
strip at the top of a page rather than its own card.

## Anatomy

An optional header row shows `caption` (small, uppercase, left) and `hint`
(monospace, right-aligned — e.g. an agent count and period). Below it, a
grid of stage cells, one per entry in `stages`, each divided by a vertical
rule: the stage's uppercase `label`, a large `value`, an optional `note`
line, and a thin progress rule filled to `progress` (0–1, with a minimum
visible sliver even near zero). `compact` tightens the cell padding and font
size for use as a page-top strip rather than a full card.

## Accessibility

`StageFlow` is built from plain `div`s with no ARIA role connecting the
stages as an ordered sequence and no `role="progressbar"` or
`aria-valuenow` on the progress rule — order and fill fraction are
communicated visually only. Tone isn't color-only: `alert` and `idle` stages
also carry a different `value` and typically a `note` explaining why (e.g.
"stalled — median age 11 days"), so always pass a `note` alongside
`tone="alert"` rather than relying on the amber tint by itself to carry the
meaning. If the stage sequence itself is information someone depends on —
not just a visual summary — make sure it's also stated in nearby text, since
the component gives assistive technology no structural cue that these cells
are steps in an order.
