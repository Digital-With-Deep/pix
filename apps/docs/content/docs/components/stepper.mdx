title: Stepper

Stepper shows where someone is in a fixed, ordered flow — an onboarding wizard, a multi-page
form — as a row of labeled bars. Earlier steps read as done, the current one is emphasized,
and later ones stay neutral until they're reached.

## When to use

- For a sequential process with a known, fixed number of steps the user moves through in
  order — account setup, a multi-step form, a checkout flow.
- Keep `steps` short and each label to one or two words; long labels truncate rather than
  wrap, since the bar above each label sets its width.
- Pass `onStepClick` only when going back is actually safe — Stepper never makes a future
  step clickable, only completed ones.
- Not for a checklist of independent, unordered actions — use `GettingStarted`, where each
  step tracks its own `done` state rather than a single `current` position.

## Basic

Each step gets a thin bar above its label: filled and marked "DONE" for steps before
`current`, dark for the current step, and neutral for steps still ahead.

## Clickable steps

Pass `onStepClick` to let people jump back to a step they've already completed — useful when
a wizard allows editing earlier answers. Future steps never become clickable, regardless of
whether `onStepClick` is set.

## Loading

Pass `loading` while the step count or current position isn't known yet. The component
renders a skeleton bar per step instead of labels that would otherwise jump once resolved.

## Anatomy

- `steps` — short step names, in order. Keep each to one or two words.
- `current` — the zero-based index of the step in progress. Every index before it reads as
  done.
- `onStepClick(index)` — when given, completed steps become clickable so people can go back.
  Future steps are never clickable, even when this is set.

## Accessibility

- The current step carries `aria-current="step"` on its list item, so assistive technology
  can announce progress without relying on the bar's color alone.
- Completed steps show the word "DONE" above the label, not just a color change, so the
  distinction holds for users who can't perceive the color difference between the filled and
  neutral bars.
- When `onStepClick` is set, completed steps become click targets on the `<li>` itself rather
  than a native `<button>` — if keyboard access to "go back" matters for your flow, pair the
  bar with an actual keyboard-operable control (for example a visible "Back" button) rather
  than relying on the step click alone.
- While `loading`, the rendered skeleton has no text content; keep loading windows short so a
  screen reader isn't left announcing nothing.
